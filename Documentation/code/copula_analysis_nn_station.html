Scientific Methodology & Computational Pipeline

Formal scientific documentation detailing the ordered computational workflow, statistical methodologies, extreme value parameterizations, copula dependence formulations, and goodness-of-fit validation protocols for non-stationary bivariate drought risk assessment.

1. Pipeline Execution Workflow

The execution flow of copula_analysis_nn_station.py proceeds sequentially through ten distinct computational stages to ingest event data, fit non-stationary univariate marginals, evaluate bivariate copula dependence structures, and project joint return periods across climate scenarios:

  1. Data Ingestion, Domain Cleaning, & Non-Finite Sanitization

    Loads raw drought event tables (dry_events.csv) and model evaluation metadata (gcm_evaluation.csv), applying strict finite domain masks (np.isfinite) to eliminate computational infinity and null artifacts across duration, intensity, and start year fields.

  2. GCM Evaluation Filtering & SPEI Growth Season Windowing

    Filters out non-scenario validation runs, excludes minor 1-month noise events (retaining events with duration > 1 month), verifies individual GCM suitability per spatial unit and SPEI timescale via Kolmogorov-Smirnov evaluation metadata, and restricts short-term indices (SPEI-3) to growth season months (March–September).

  3. Temporal Partitioning & Annual Occurrence Rate ($\lambda$ / $E$) Calculation

    Divides drought records into reference baseline ($Y \in [\text{ref_start}, \text{ref_end}]$) and projection future ($Y \in [\text{proj_start}, \text{proj_end}]$) periods, computing the baseline mean annual event arrival rate ($E_{\text{ref}} = N_{\text{ref}} / \Delta Y_{\text{ref}}$) and future arrival rate ($E_{\text{proj}} = N_{\text{proj}} / \Delta Y_{\text{proj}}$).

  4. Univariate Marginal Distribution Fitting & Non-Stationary Drift Optimization

    Fits stationary distributions for the historical baseline and non-stationary time-dependent distributions (where location $\mu(t) = \mu_0 + \mu_1 t$ or scale $\sigma(t) = \sigma_0(1 + \sigma_1 t)$ evolves linearly with normalized time $t \in [0, 1]$ and loc = 0.0 fixed) for future projections across candidate families (GEV, GPD, Gamma, Lognormal, Weibull, Pearson Type III, Exponential, and Normal) using L-BFGS-B maximum likelihood estimation.

  5. Discrete Dither Tie-Breaking, Anderson-Darling GoF Testing, & 3-Tier Hierarchical Selection

    Applies deterministic uniform dithering ($\pm 0.49$) on discrete duration integer ties, transforms observations to pseudo-uniform space $U_t = F(X_t; \hat{\theta}(t))$, evaluates goodness-of-fit (GoF) using Anderson-Darling test statistics with $n_{\text{mc}} = 500$ Monte Carlo simulations, and selects optimal models via a 3-Tier Hierarchical Gating Protocol (Tier 1: $p > 0.05$ lowest BIC; Tier 2: $0.01 < p \le 0.05$ lowest BIC; Tier 3: fallback to lowest BIC with poor fit warning).

  6. Probability Integral Transform (PIT) to Uniform Pseudo-Observations

    Transforms marginal drought duration ($D$) and absolute severity intensity ($I$) observations into uniform pseudo-observations $(u, v) \in [0, 1]^2$ using their respective fitted cumulative distribution functions: $u_i = F_D(D_i)$ and $v_i = F_I(I_i)$, clipped to $[10^{-5}, 1 - 10^{-5}]$.

  7. Bivariate Copula Selection & Joint Dependence Fitting

    Fits bivariate copula models using pyvinecopulib. Performs automated candidate selection across 11 families (Gaussian, Student-t, Clayton, Gumbel, Frank, Joe, BB1, BB6, BB7, BB8, Tawn) based on Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC), or fits a specified copula family (e.g., Gumbel) to capture joint tail dependence structures.

  8. Historical Univariate Threshold Quantification for Target Return Periods

    For each target return period $T \in \{1, 5, 10, 30, 50, 100\}$ years, calculates the non-exceedance probability per event $p_{\text{non_exceed}} = 1 - 1 / (T \cdot E_{\text{ref}})$, and derives corresponding historical univariate duration ($d_{\text{thresh}}$) and intensity ($i_{\text{thresh}}$) quantile thresholds via inverse cumulative distribution functions ($F^{-1}$).

  9. Bivariate Joint Exceedance Probability & Joint Return Period Derivation

    Evaluates joint survival probabilities $P(D > d_{\text{thresh}} \land I > i_{\text{thresh}}) = 1 - u - v + C(u, v)$ for both reference and projection periods, and translates joint probabilities into joint return periods in years: $T_{\text{joint, ref}} = 1 / (P_{\text{joint, ref}} \cdot E_{\text{ref}})$ and $T_{\text{joint, proj}} = 1 / (P_{\text{joint, proj}} \cdot E_{\text{proj}}$).

  10. Likelihood Change Ratio Calculation & Composite Reliability Scoring

    Quantifies drought hazard intensification via the Likelihood Change ratio $LC = T_{\text{joint, ref}} / T_{\text{joint, proj}}$, computes an aggregate composite marginal reliability score (sum of 4 GoF scores, max 8), and exports formatted results to CSV (nuts_2_copula_analysis.csv).

2. Brief Summary of the Method

Methodological Summary for Publication: We present a non-stationary bivariate extreme value copula modeling framework designed to assess climate-driven shifts in joint drought hazard characteristics across European spatial units under various Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs). Droughts are represented as coupled bivariate processes characterized by duration ($D$, consecutive months with $\text{SPEI} < \text{threshold}$) and severity intensity ($I = |\text{SPEI}|$). To capture temporal non-stationarity induced by ongoing global warming, univariate marginal distributions for future projection horizons incorporate time-covariate parameter drifts optimized via Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE), whereas historical baselines are represented by stationary formulations. Candidate marginals—spanning Generalized Extreme Value (GEV), Generalized Pareto (GPD), Gamma, Lognormal, Weibull, Pearson Type III, Exponential, and Normal distributions—are evaluated using a non-parametric Anderson-Darling test with 500 Monte Carlo simulations on Uniform-projected pseudo-observations. Model selection is governed by a 3-Tier Hierarchical Protocol that enforces statistical significance ($p > 0.05$) prior to Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) parsimony ranking, preventing small-sample likelihood singularities without resorting to p-hacking. Bivariate dependence structures are modeled using Archimedean, Elliptical, and Extreme-Value copula families (e.g., Gumbel, Clayton, Joe, Tawn, BB-series) selected via BIC. Bivariate joint return periods ($T_{\text{joint}}$) are derived using survival copulas under historical quantile thresholds, allowing direct quantification of future risk amplification via the Likelihood Change ratio ($LC = T_{\text{joint, ref}} / T_{\text{joint, proj}}$) alongside a composite statistical reliability score.

3. Scientific Method Details by Workflow Stage

The following detailed scientific descriptions expand upon each pipeline operation for inclusion in methodology sections of peer-reviewed journal publications.

Stage 01 Data Ingestion, Domain Cleaning, & Non-Finite Sanitization

Raw drought event series derived from monthly Standardized Precipitation-Evapotranspiration Index (SPEI) time series are ingested alongside General Circulation Model (GCM) evaluation performance tables. Upstream hydrological operations, particularly ratio-based intensity metrics, occasionally produce floating-point division anomalies resulting in positive or negative computational infinities ($\pm \infty$). To prevent numerical instability, matrix broadcasting failures, or optimization divergence during maximum likelihood estimation, a strict multi-variable bitwise mask is enforced: $$\mathcal{M}_{\text{valid}} = \left\{ i \in \{1, \dots, N\} \mid \text{is_finite}(D_i) \land D_i > 0 \land \text{is_finite}(I_i) \land \text{is_finite}(Y_i) \right\}$$ Only observations satisfying $\mathcal{M}_{\text{valid}}$ simultaneously across duration $D$, intensity $I$, and event start year $Y$ are admitted into marginal fitting routines.

Stage 02 GCM Evaluation Filtering & SPEI Growth Season Windowing

Multi-model ensembles are subjected to regional model quality control. GCM simulations for specific spatial units and SPEI timescales are evaluated against observation-based baseline datasets (e.g., CRU TS) using two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests. GCM runs failing critical performance thresholds are disqualified (gcm_is_valid = False) to eliminate biased driving climate inputs. Furthermore, isolated single-month dry spells ($D = 1$ month) are removed to exclude transient sub-monthly dry noise that does not represent sustained agricultural or hydrological drought. For short-term agricultural drought indices ($\text{SPEI-3}$), event evaluation is strictly bounded to the vegetation growth season (March to September, months 3–9) to isolate periods of active evapotranspirative demand.

Stage 03 Temporal Partitioning & Annual Arrival Rate Calculation

Cleaned drought event series are partitioned into a historical reference window ($Y_{\text{ref}} \in [\text{ref_start}, \text{ref_end}]$, e.g., 1950–2010) and a future projection window ($Y_{\text{proj}} \in [\text{proj_start}, \text{proj_end}]$, e.g., 2030–2100). The mean annual drought occurrence rate $\lambda$ (events per year, $E$) is computed independently for each temporal partition as: $$E_{\text{ref}} = \frac{N_{\text{ref}}}{Y_{\text{ref, max}} - Y_{\text{ref, min}} + 1}, \quad E_{\text{proj}} = \frac{N_{\text{proj}}}{Y_{\text{proj, max}} - Y_{\text{proj, min}} + 1}$$ where $N_{\text{ref}}$ and $N_{\text{proj}}$ denote the total count of valid drought events recorded in each respective window. Annual event frequencies serve as crucial scaling parameters when converting per-event exceedance probabilities into chronological return periods in years.

Stage 04 Univariate Marginal Distribution Fitting & Non-Stationary Drift Optimization

Univariate marginal distributions are fitted independently to drought duration ($D$) and absolute severity intensity ($I = |\text{SPEI}|$). For historical reference periods (is_reference_period = True), both stationary and non-stationary distribution models are fitted and allowed to compete. For future projection periods (is_reference_period = False), non-stationary time-dependent models are strictly engaged to capture transient climate change signals. Eight parametric candidate distributions are evaluated: Generalized Extreme Value (genextreme), Generalized Pareto (genpareto), Gamma (gamma), Lognormal (lognorm), Weibull minimum (weibull_min), Pearson Type III (pearson3), Exponential (expon), and Normal (norm).

Time non-stationarity is modeled by introducing a normalized time covariate $t \in [0, 1]$, where $t_i = (Y_i - Y_{\text{min}}) / (Y_{\text{max}} - Y_{\text{min}})$. Parameter dynamics are formulated as: $$\text{GEV Location Drift:} \quad \mu(t) = \mu_0 + \mu_1 t, \quad \sigma = \text{const}$$ $$\text{Scale Drift (GPD, Gamma, Lognorm, Weibull, Pearson III, Expon):} \quad \sigma(t) = \sigma_0 (1 + \sigma_1 t), \quad \text{loc} = 0.0 \text{ (fixed)}$$ Fixing $\text{loc} = 0.0$ for non-negative physical quantities ($D, I > 0$) prevents threshold singularities where numerical optimizers shift location parameters onto sample minima to artificially inflate log-likelihood. Parameter estimation is performed via maximum likelihood optimization using the L-BFGS-B algorithm with bounded constraints (e.g., shape $c \in [-0.5, 0.5]$ for GEV/GPD to ensure finite variance).

Stage 05 Discrete Dither Tie-Breaking, Anderson-Darling GoF, & 3-Tier Hierarchical Selection

Drought duration is natively recorded as discrete integer months, creating ties in empirical cumulative density. To prevent tie-induced distortion during continuous Probability Integral Transforms, a deterministic uniform dither $d_i \sim \mathcal{U}(-0.49, 0.49)$ is added to duration values ($D_i' = \max(D_i + d_i, 0.1)$) using a fixed random seed (seed = 42) for strict computational reproducibility.

Goodness-of-fit (GoF) is evaluated by transforming observed data into pseudo-observations $U_i = F(X_i \mid \hat{\theta}(t_i))$ via the fitted cumulative distribution function. If the fitted model accurately represents the data, $U_i$ follows a standard Uniform distribution $\mathcal{U}(0, 1)$. Hypothesis testing is conducted using the Anderson-Darling statistic ($A^2$) evaluated via $n_{\text{mc}} = 500$ Monte Carlo simulations: $$A^2 = -n - \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{2i - 1}{n} \left[ \ln(U_{(i)}) + \ln(1 - U_{(n - i + 1)}) \right]$$

Model selection is governed by a strict 3-Tier Hierarchical Protocol that prioritizes statistical validity before parsimony ranking:

  • Tier 1 (Significant Pool): Selects the model with the lowest Bayesian Information Criterion ($\text{BIC} = k \ln(n) - 2 \ln \hat{L}$) among all candidate models achieving $p > 0.05$ (assigned GoF Fit Score = 2).
  • Tier 2 (Acceptable Pool): If zero models achieve $p > 0.05$, selects the lowest BIC model among those with $0.01 < p \le 0.05$ (assigned GoF Fit Score = 1).
  • Tier 3 (Unfit Data Guard / No P-Hacking): If all models yield $p \le 0.01$, selects the lowest BIC model, logs a poor fit warning, and assigns GoF Fit Score = 0 without forcing artificial statistical convergence.

Stage 06 Probability Integral Transform (PIT) to Uniform Pseudo-Observations

Following univariate marginal selection, observed duration ($D_i$) and intensity ($I_i$) samples are mapped into uniform pseudo-observation space $[0, 1]^2$ via the Probability Integral Transform (PIT): $$u_i = F_D(D_i \mid \hat{\theta}_D(t_i)), \quad v_i = F_I(I_i \mid \hat{\theta}_I(t_i))$$ where $F_D$ and $F_I$ are the selected parametric CDFs evaluated at observation-specific time covariates $t_i$. Derived uniform variates are numerically clipped to $[10^{-5}, 1 - 10^{-5}]$ to eliminate boundary overflow errors during copula density evaluations.

Stage 07 Bivariate Copula Selection & Joint Dependence Fitting

According to Sklar's Theorem, the joint cumulative distribution function $H(d, i)$ of coupled drought duration and intensity can be uniquely expressed via a bivariate copula $C:[0, 1]^2 \to [0, 1]$: $$H(d, i) = C\left( F_D(d), F_I(i) \right)$$ Bivariate copula models are fitted using the pyvinecopulib C++ numerical library. When automated copula selection is enabled (copula_family = None), candidate copulas are evaluated across 11 families: Gaussian, Student-t, Clayton, Gumbel, Frank, Joe, BB1, BB6, BB7, BB8, and Tawn. The optimal copula family is selected based on BIC. When a specific family is mandated (e.g., Gumbel), the parameter $\theta$ is estimated via Maximum Likelihood Estimation directly on uniform pseudo-observations $(u_i, v_i)$. Kendall's rank correlation ($\tau$) and empirical upper tail dependence ($\lambda_U$) are extracted to quantify asymptotic co-occurrence of extreme duration and intensity events.

Stage 08 Univariate Exceedance Threshold Determination for Return Periods

To evaluate joint hazards under climate change, baseline univariate thresholds are established for standard design return periods $T \in \{1, 5, 10, 30, 50, 100\}$ years. The per-event exceedance probability $p_{\text{exceed}}$ corresponding to a $T$-year return period is defined as: $$p_{\text{exceed}} = \frac{1}{T \cdot E_{\text{ref}}}, \quad p_{\text{non_exceed}} = 1 - p_{\text{exceed}}$$ Historical univariate quantile thresholds for duration ($d_{\text{thresh}}$) and intensity ($i_{\text{thresh}}$) are calculated by applying the inverse cumulative distribution function (percent point function, PPF) of the historical reference marginals at $p_{\text{non_exceed}}$: $$d_{\text{thresh}} = F_{D, \text{ref}}^{-1}(p_{\text{non_exceed}}), \quad i_{\text{thresh}} = F_{I, \text{ref}}^{-1}(p_{\text{non_exceed}})$$ These historical thresholds represent fixed physical hazard levels against which future joint probabilities are compared.

Stage 09 Bivariate Joint Probability & Joint Return Period Derivation

Joint exceedance probabilities under the "AND" survival hazard scenario (where both duration $D$ exceeds $d_{\text{thresh}}$ AND intensity $I$ exceeds $i_{\text{thresh}}$ simultaneously) are calculated using the bivariate survival copula formulation: $$P(D > d_{\text{thresh}} \land I > i_{\text{thresh}}) = 1 - u - v + C(u, v)$$ where $u = F_D(d_{\text{thresh}})$ and $v = F_I(i_{\text{thresh}})$.

For the historical reference period, marginal CDFs and baseline copula $C_{\text{ref}}$ yield joint probability $P_{\text{joint, ref}}$, which is scaled into historical joint return period in years: $$T_{\text{joint, ref}} = \frac{1}{P_{\text{joint, ref}} \cdot E_{\text{ref}}}$$ For future projection periods, marginal CDFs evaluated at the final simulation horizon time covariate $t_{\text{proj_end}}$ and projected copula $C_{\text{proj}}$ yield future joint probability $P_{\text{joint, proj}}$ for the same historical thresholds $(d_{\text{thresh}}, i_{\text{thresh}})$. The future joint return period is derived as: $$T_{\text{joint, proj}} = \frac{1}{P_{\text{joint, proj}} \cdot E_{\text{proj}}}$$

Stage 10 Likelihood Change Ratio & Composite Reliability Score Computation

The relative change in drought hazard frequency under future climate conditions is expressed by the Likelihood Change ratio ($LC$): $$LC = \frac{T_{\text{joint, ref}}}{T_{\text{joint, proj}}}$$ An $LC > 1.0$ indicates an increased hazard frequency (e.g., an $LC = 2.5$ means a historical 50-year joint drought event will recur $2.5\times$ more frequently in the future, corresponding to a reduced future return period $T_{\text{joint, proj}} = 20$ years).

To assess model confidence across the 4 underlying marginal distribution fits (Reference Duration, Reference Intensity, Projection Duration, Projection Intensity), a composite Reliability Score ($R \in [0, 8]$) is calculated: $$R = S_{\text{dur, ref}} + S_{\text{int, ref}} + S_{\text{dur, proj}} + S_{\text{int, proj}}$$ where each individual fit score $S \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ represents Poor ($p \le 0.01$), Acceptable ($0.01 < p \le 0.05$), or Superior ($p > 0.05$) Anderson-Darling statistical significance. An aggregate score of $R = 8$ confirms uncompromised statistical reliability across all marginal components.

5. Statistical & Computational Safeguards

To ensure robust automated execution across thousands of spatial GCM projections without manual intervention, the pipeline incorporates strict mathematical shields.

A. Domain Sanitization Shield

Upstream division approaching zero can yield computational floating-point infinities (intensity = np.inf). Explicit non-finite masks are enforced at all entry checkpoints:

valid_mask = np.isfinite(df['duration']) & (df['duration'] > 0) & np.isfinite(df['intensity']) & np.isfinite(df['start_year'])
events_df = events_df[valid_mask].copy()

B. Location Parameter Pinning (loc = 0.0)

Drought duration and intensity are strictly non-negative physical quantities ($D, I > 0$). Unconstrained location parameters enable numerical optimizers to shift location onto sample minima, generating artificial likelihood spikes. Pinning loc = 0.0 for GPD, Gamma, Lognormal, Weibull, Pearson III, and Exponential models eliminates boundary singularities.
